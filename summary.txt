World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. Ukraine and Russia ended two days of US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi agreeing in principle to a large prisoner-of-war exchange, but no ceasefire or deal on territory; US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff head to Moscow next (Reuters; AP). NATO jets scrambled after at least four drones strayed into Baltic airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia (Reuters). In the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's army said a tanker caught fire after striking a naval mine east of the strait — not independently confirmed — while parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said it would never return to its pre-war status (Tasnim; Al Jazeera). Israeli strikes killed at least seven people in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said; Israel's military said it had struck militants (Reuters). At COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres told world leaders the climate crisis is "outrunning us", on "a path to mutually assured destruction", as leaders began two days of speeches (Reuters). In Washington, House Republicans blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, voting 211-204 to refer the measure to committee, and a federal judge let President Donald Trump's defamation lawsuit against the BBC proceed (Axios; Politico). The US Justice Department announced charges against three alleged Iran-backed operatives over plots in the US and Europe, including at RAF Fairford in Britain (BBC). DR Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,042 confirmed cases and 5,387 deaths, government data showed (Reuters). China's January-October trade surplus reached $1.04 trillion, with October exports up 35.2% year on year (Reuters). In Seongnam, South Korea, a city bus hit 32 vehicles in the morning rush hour, seriously injuring nine people and lightly injuring 48 (Reuters). A UK court struck down Trinidad and Tobago's colonial-era ban on gay sex (AP).
